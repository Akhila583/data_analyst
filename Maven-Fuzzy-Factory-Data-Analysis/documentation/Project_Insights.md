# Project Insights

## 1. Website Performance

Website traffic and order volume increased significantly over the analysis period.

The monthly conversion rate improved from approximately 3% in early 2012 to more than 8% by March 2015, indicating substantial improvement in the website's ability to convert visitors into customers.

Revenue per session also increased over time, indicating that the growth was not driven only by higher traffic.

---

## 2. Marketing Performance

Marketing sources showed significant differences in traffic, conversion, and revenue.

### Key observations

- Gsearch was the strongest marketing source by overall traffic and revenue.
- Different marketing sources produced different conversion rates.
- Revenue per session was used to evaluate the quality of traffic rather than relying only on session volume.

### Business implication

Marketing investment should consider both traffic volume and conversion performance when evaluating acquisition channels.

---

## 3. Device Performance

Desktop generated substantially more website sessions, orders, and revenue than mobile.

However, mobile showed different conversion behavior compared with desktop.

### Business implication

The business should continue monitoring the mobile customer journey and identify opportunities to improve mobile conversion and user experience.

---

## 4. Landing Page Performance

Landing page performance varied considerably.

The analysis identified significant differences in conversion rates across:

- `/home`
- `/lander-1`
- `/lander-2`
- `/lander-3`
- `/lander-4`
- `/lander-5`

The highest-performing landing pages can provide useful benchmarks for improving lower-performing pages.

### Business implication

Landing pages with stronger conversion rates can be studied for differences in messaging, layout, product presentation, and calls to action.

---

## 5. Product Performance

The Original Mr. Fuzzy was the highest-revenue product among the analyzed products.

Product-level analysis was performed using:

- Orders
- Revenue
- Cost
- Profit
- Profit margin
- Refunds

This allowed product performance to be evaluated beyond revenue alone.

---

## 6. Product Profitability

Profitability varied across products.

Profit was calculated as:

Profit = Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold

Profit margin was calculated as:

Profit Margin = Profit / Revenue × 100

This helps identify products that generate strong financial returns rather than simply high sales.

---

## 7. Refund Analysis

Refund amounts were analyzed at the product level to identify products associated with higher refund values.

Refund analysis can help the business investigate potential issues related to:

- Product quality
- Customer expectations
- Product descriptions
- Order fulfillment
- Customer experience

---

## 8. Product Combinations

Product combinations were analyzed using order-level self-joins to identify products frequently purchased together.

This analysis can support:

- Cross-selling
- Product bundling
- Upselling
- Recommendation strategies

---

## 9. Overall Business Insights

The analysis demonstrates that business performance should be evaluated across multiple dimensions rather than using revenue alone.

The key dimensions analyzed were:

- Traffic
- Orders
- Conversion rate
- Revenue
- Revenue per session
- Profit
- Profit margin
- Marketing source
- Device type
- Landing page
- Product
- Refunds

The Power BI dashboard brings these metrics together to provide an interactive view of overall e-commerce performance.
